10.076-4 Tooth Segment (10 Teeth, RW) — dimensional audit and 3× reference

Scope and source status

  • Part: 10.076, Zahnsegment (10 Zähne) für Resultatzählwerk (tooth segment (10 Teeth, RW)), drawing 10.076-4, current; replaces 10.076-3. Identity verified against Drawing-Index.md: page 100 is 10.076-4, and the sheet's own title block agrees. Scale 5:1; stamped Abmasse in μ = 1/1000 mm.
  • Primary source: original Contina engineering drawing, drawing-set PDF page 100 / drawing-viewer page 100, rasterized at 200–400 dpi.
  • Parent(s) and quantity: 10.213 Complete Step Drum; 1 per machine (Type I; the master of the RW family). Material: Nickel silver (Neusilber) 0.5 −20/−40 mm, hard-rolled, Brinell 230–240. General tolerance: ±50 µm, ±30′. Runout: 20 µm marked radial and side; none marked. Drawn: 12.1.50. Revisions: index 3→4 (change 139, 26.10.50).
  • Labels: Confirmed (printed), Derived (calculated), Unverified, Inferred; 3× values are Derived project references, not factory specifications.

Official standards references: ISO 286-1 and ISO 286-2.

What the 55° callouts measure (Derived, measured on the 400 dpi scan by tools/measure_55deg_10076.py; 2026-10-21). Left callout: the lower thin line of the arc lies on a ray from the bore centre (it passes 0.09 mm from the fitted centre and is within 0.4° of radial; it is 28.0° from straight down). The upper thin line runs out of the end tooth's long release ramp and makes 55.7° with that ray (printed 55°). It makes 67.5° with the heavy drive face, so the 55° is not the angle between the two flanks bounding a tooth gap, and the lower line is not an extension of a tooth flank (the drive face of that tooth is about 12° off the ray). The 55° is therefore the release ramp's angle to a radial ray through the end tooth's corner. What the ray itself is is Unverified: it is not one of the 60° construction rays (those meet 5.0 mm above the bore centre, see below), and it is not an end ray of the 101°15′ span; it passes within 0.7° of the seventh tooth's tip. Right callout (tools/measure_55deg_right_10076.py): one side is a radial ray from the bore centre (0.11 mm from the centre, 51.2° from straight down); the other is a near-vertical thin line (4.6° from vertical, 8.8 mm from the centre, so not radial) that is collinear with a heavy near-vertical outline face under the right-hand tab (0.00 mm off its extension, 1.3° apart). They make 55.8° (printed 55°). So the right callout also pairs an extended heavy outline face with a radial ray, but the face is a near-vertical face at the segment's right end, about 45° off the local radial, and is not shown to be a tooth flank (the traced drive faces are about 8° off radial); whether it is the segment's end face is Unverified. (2026-10-24: the radial ray at 51.2° is read as the ray of a virtual tooth one pitch beyond tooth 1; see "Tooth placement" below.) The two rays sit 28.0° left and 51.2° right of straight down, 79.2° apart, so they are not the two ends of the 101°15′ span. Earlier notes called both callouts end/dovetail-face angles (Drum-Assembly-10213.md, Machine-Facts.md); that is contradicted for the left callout by this measurement. A 2026-10-21 working reading, "the angle between tooth 7's and tooth 8's flanks", is retracted: the user's own suspicion that the line came from the nine-pitch dimension's ray was the right one to test, and the line is a radial ray, not a flank.

Terms used here (not printed on the sheet). Flank: either face of a tooth. Drive face: the steep flank, on the left of each tooth as drawn (traced 7.6° off radial). Release ramp: the long flank, on the right of each tooth as drawn (traced 49.8° off radial on average; the 55° callout reads the end tooth's ramp against the ray). Tooth gap: the space between adjacent teeth. "Leading" and "trailing" are avoided because they depend on the (unverified) direction of travel.

Tooth root fillet. 0,5r is a 0.5 mm radius (1.5 mm at 3×) with no tolerance printed. Its arrow points at the concave corner between teeth, where the flank meets the land. Check on the trace (Derived): the eight root corners of the ten-tooth outline fit local radii of 0.46–0.61 mm (mean 0.52), at r ≈ 10.9 mm, i.e. the land radius.

The 60° construction angle has its own origin (the user's reading, confirmed on the scan 2026-10-21). The two 60° lines run from the apex down through the two shoulders, and with the 13.18 shoulder chord they form an equilateral triangle: apex angle 60° and equal sides give three 60° angles, sides 13.18 mm, height 13.18 × √3/2 = 11.414 mm. The shoulders sit on the R9.2 bore at 6.59 mm either side of the axis and √(9.2² − 6.59²) = 6.420 mm below the bore centre (a shoulder-to-centre triangle has a 91.5° apex, not 60°), so the 60° apex is 11.414 − 6.420 = 4.994 mm above the bore centre, not at it (Derived; the shoulder identity of the chord ends is Unverified). Measured on the 400 dpi scan: the two lines are 30.3° and 30.0° from vertical, each passes 0.08 mm from the predicted shoulder, they meet 5.08 mm above the bore centre and make 60.3° with each other. The 125° arc, by contrast, is concentric with the bore (fitted centre 0.1 mm from it, radius 20.33 mm). An earlier note here, that the 60° rays pass through the bore centre, was wrong: the lines measured were other construction lines.

The single pitch and the total are separate dimensions. The single pitch is the angle between matching features of two adjacent teeth; the total runs from a feature on the first tooth to the same feature on the last. Ten teeth have nine gaps, so the total is 9 pitches, not 10 (Confirmed, both printed). The ±15′ is printed on each, so the nine-pitch span is held to ±15′ as a whole: pitch errors may not accumulate along the arc, and in CAD the teeth should come from one polar pattern (10 occurrences over 9 × 11.25° = 101.25°), not from chained angles. Which two edges the single-pitch arrows touch is not stated on the sheet (Unverified; the project's reading is matching, e.g. trailing, edges of adjacent teeth). The remaining 23°45′ of the 125° blank is Derived (125 − 101.25); the 12.5° lead-in reading that accounts for part of it is worked in Drum-Assembly-10213.md.

Where the tolerance column says "general", the limits column applies the sheet's general tolerance (a Derived reading of a printed general note).

Why the two callouts are kept separate. The sheet prints 9,2r ±20 (symmetric) and 2,9 0/−50 (one-sided) as independent dimensions. No sum is dimensioned or toleranced on the sheet, so the 12.030–12.120 mm range above is a worst-case stack-up of two callouts, not a drawing requirement. It also assumes the 2.9 arrows start on the 9.2 arc, which is Inferred from the drawing.

What the 2.9 controls (the user's reading, now supported by a measurement). The 2,9 0/−50 dimension line runs along the same line as the 9,2r ±20 bore radius and its outer arrowhead ends on a thin circle. Measured on the 400 dpi scan, where that thin arc is clear of heavy strokes (−72° to −64.5° from straight down), it lies at R12.07–12.10 mm (median 12.09), which matches 9.2 + 2.9 = 12.1 (Derived). The user reads that circle as the one the tooth tips lie on, so the stack is the raw tip-circle radius (12.030–12.120 mm). On the scan the tips visibly touch that circle. The user also points to the thin arc that runs along the bottom tips (straight below the origin, above the 10 chord) and continues beyond the last tip; at 400 dpi it lies at R12.06 mm (median of 16 clear samples, noisy) with the two lowest tip strokes lying just inside it, their outer edges on it. The outline trace puts the tip stroke centre-lines at R12.004 (11.993–12.020), 0.06–0.09 mm inside the thin circle, i.e. about half a stroke width: the tooth outline is drawn up to the circle, not centred on it. Taken together the tips lie on the R12.1 circle (9.2 + 2.9) to within about 0.05 mm (Inferred, supported by two independent places: the tab end of the 2.9 and the bottom tips). The finished tooth envelope is the Ø24 ±20 µm on 10.213-2, i.e. R12.0, which would be a finishing allowance of about 0.1 mm in radius below the raw circle (Machine-Facts §2).

Tooth placement: where tooth 1 goes (project derivation, 2026-10-24)

The sheet prints the pitch (11°15′), the first-to-last span (9 × 11°15′ = 101°15′) and the blank arc (125°). It prints no distance from either end of the part to the nearest tooth, and does not say where the pitch rays cut a tooth. The chain below fixes tooth 1 for the 3× model. Angles are measured from straight down, positive toward the right end (the end with the first tooth). The 23.28° choice rests on one reading. If the left end line does not touch tooth 10's tip corner, or the sheet's rays meet the teeth somewhere other than the middle of the flat, the offset changes. The size of the change is the 0.47° half flat (0.3 mm at 3× on the tip circle). The 3× model should carry the value as a named parameter (first_tooth_offset) so it changes in one place.

Ray 0 and the 55° callout (the author's reading, checked against the scan). One pitch beyond tooth 1 toward the right end is the ray of a virtual tooth 0: 23.277° − 11.25° from the end ray, i.e. 50.47° right of straight down. The right-hand 55° callout was measured on the scan against a radial ray at 51.2° (tools/measure_55deg_right_10076.py), 0.7° from it. On this reading the 55° is the angle between tooth 1's release ramp and ray 0, the V of a tooth gap whose far side is tooth 0's drive face, taken parallel to ray 0. That puts the ramp 55° − 11.25° = 43.75° off tooth 1's own radial ray and about 4.5° from vertical.

The family this sheet defines

Nine other RW sheets (10.078–10.090, except 10.076) and the two UW sheets say Aus 10.076-3 angefertigt ("made from 10.076-3"); each has its own audit. The derived segments keep the master's bore, tabs and outline and drop the first (10 − n) teeth. This is the only sheet with the full tolerance set.

Plate thickness

0,5 −20/−40 here (0.460–0.480 mm); the nine RW derived sheets and the two UW sheets read 0,5 −0/−20 (0.480–0.500). See any derived audit, e.g. Part-Dimensions-10078.md. The revision table records only Index 3 in 4 geändert, change 139, 26.10.50; what it changed is not written (Unverified), and the derived sheets were not reissued. The parts list agrees with the derived sheets: 10.210 sheets 4 and 5 give all twelve segments (the master included) as 0.5 −0.02/0 or +0/−0.02, so 10.076-4 is the outlier; with −0.020/−0.040 the 37-plate stack could not reach the stations on 10.213-2 (see Part-Dimensions-10213.md).

Hardness

Hard-rolled nickel silver, Brinell 230–240 (as printed). No hardening is specified; the segments are cold-worked strip.
