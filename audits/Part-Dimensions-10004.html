Arm angles. Printed at 20° steps below the horizontal centre line — 20°, 40°, 60°, 80°, 100°, 120°, 140°, 160°, 180°, 200° — and at 30°, 50°, 70°, 90°, 110°, 130° above it. Read as angles from the right-hand horizontal arm, this places the arms at 0°, 30°, 50°, 70°, 90°, 110°, 130° upward, then the arm printed as 200° (equal to 160° the other way), 180° and eight more down to 20°: 17 arms (a count of the drawn arms, Derived). That is the number of Ø2 balls 10.201 carries at its arm stations (17, BOM sheet 9 total 26), which is why the sheet is read as one arm per ball. The two 30° gaps (0°→30° and 130°→160°, both measured upward) are the only places the 20° pitch is broken; the reason is not stated (Unverified).

Derived. A 3× table is not a factory specification. See the project's tolerance strategy. Two caution notes, both Derived engineering estimates and not tested: a spring's force does not scale with the geometry alone. For a bending arm of width w, thickness t and length L, stiffness goes as w·t³/L³, so geometrically tripling everything triples the force at the same relative deflection. And a 0.3 mm strip is not a 0.1 mm strip that has been made bigger: the material choice and hardness (230–240 Brinell here) must be re-derived for a 3× part rather than carried over.
