The spiral is a 10-digit cam (Derived)

The axle carries nine pockets along a helical band, 36° apart in angle (36°, 72°, …, 324°, each ±1°) and 2 mm apart along the axle (2, 4, …, 18 mm, each ±0.020). The spiral pitch is 20 mm per 360°, so 36° corresponds to 20 × 36/360 = 2.000 mm: the angular and axial ladders agree exactly (Derived check). Together with the countersink notch at the top, these are the ten digit positions 0–9 of one setting slider: turning the axle by 36° per digit moves the pocket engaged by the drum's tooth stack.

Quantity Calculation Value
Angular pitch 360°/10 positions 36°
Axial pitch 20 mm × 36°/360° 2.0 mm
Positions on the sheet pockets 1–9 plus the 110° countersink at the top 10

The spiral's tolerance is ±0.020 mm per station against the ±1° angular limit: at the Ø2.5 surface, ±1° is ±0.022 mm of arc (Derived), so the axial and angular limits are matched in size.

Length grading (Aussortieren nach)

The axles are sorted into Pos. 1: 35.18–35.20 mm and Pos. 2: 35.20–35.22 mm, the two halves of the 35.2 ±0.020 band. Assembly-Links.csv already records that 10.212 is sorted per 10.061 Pos. 1/2 for its use in 10.240.

Fit with the digit roller 10.029 and the knob 10.057

The axle is Ø2.5 h6: pressed into the digit roller 10.029 (interference 2 to 23 µm, Part-Dimensions-10029.md) and sliding in the knob 10.057 (clearance 20 to 52 µm, Part-Dimensions-10057.md). Both are Derived pairings.

Correction

Machine-Facts §4 and §4a recorded this axle as Ø2.5 h7. Its own sheet reads Ø2,5-h6 (fit table 0/−7) and the BOM master agrees, so the earlier entry was the outlier and is corrected.

Project 3× reference

Convention used here: the project's tolerance strategy (a project decision) ties tolerance to the manufacturing process, not to the 3× factor. "Simply scaled" values are a comparison only and are not adopted, general tolerances are not silently carried over, and a 3× table is not a factory specification. ISO codes are re-applied at the scaled size; a scaled nominal exactly on a size-step boundary belongs to the lower step.
