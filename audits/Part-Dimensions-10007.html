10.007-3 Tens-Carry Spring — dimensional audit and 3× reference

Scope and source status

  • Part: 10.007, Zehnerschaltfeder (tens-carry spring), drawing revision 10.007-3
  • Primary source: original Contina engineering drawing, drawing-set PDF page 13 / drawing-viewer page 13
  • Drawing values: Confirmed from the current drawing and title block
  • 3× values: Derived project references, not factory specifications
  • ISO 286 does not apply to this part. 10.007 is a formed wire spring — it has no hole/shaft interface on this sheet, so there is no ISO 286 hole/shaft case to identify. Its tolerances are direct linear/angular callouts and the title block's general (non-ISO) tolerance block, handled per this project's tolerance strategy rather than ISO 286-1/2 (see the project's tolerance strategy: tolerance is tied to manufacturing process, not to the 3× geometric scale — factory absolute tolerance bands are held near-original rather than multiplied by 3).

Part anatomy (informal terms adopted during the OBS-007 investigation, not printed on the sheet): bridge (the connecting member), two legs running from the bridge, and a foot (bent tab) at the free end of each leg.

The base-spacing (2.2 mm) vs. foot-spacing (0.5 mm) dimensions establish that the two legs are designed to converge, not run parallel. Do not subtract these two numbers to assert 1.7 mm centerline closure: the foot value is a clear gap and the rounded bridge datum needs interpretation. Any acceptance criterion for spring geometry (see the OBS-007 spring survey) should compare against this converging-taper target, not a parallel-legs assumption.

Derived: leg convergence angle

The base-spacing, foot-spacing and leg-length dimensions together imply a per-leg lean angle off the bridge — useful as a forming control (bend to an angle) instead of trying to gauge the small foot spacing directly on a floppy wire tip. This angle is scale-invariant: since all three inputs scale by 3× together, the 1× and 3× angle are identical.

Simple centerline reading (this audit's original interpretation — base and foot spacing both taken as direct centerline-to-centerline spacing):

half-width at base = 2.200 / 2 = 1.100 mm
half-width at foot = 0.500 / 2 = 0.250 mm
inward travel per leg = 1.100 − 0.250 = 0.850 mm
run (leg length)      = 6.700 mm

angle = atan(0.850 / 6.700) ≈ 7.23°

Jig-spec's clear-gap reading (per the spring-forming jig specification's bridge_virtual_span/foot_clear_gap interpretation — foot spacing taken as a clear gap between wire surfaces, so the wire diameter is added back in for center-to-center spacing, and the run is reduced by one wire diameter):

atan((6.60 − 2.10) / (2 × 19.50)) ≈ 6.58°   (3× figures, from the jig spec §3)

Both figures are Derived, not directly stated on the drawing; the ~0.6° spread between them is a direct consequence of the same unresolved base/foot-spacing datum question noted above (centerline vs. clear gap), not a new source of uncertainty. Use whichever the jig implementation adopts as its bridge_inside_r/inclination convention, and note explicitly which one a formed prototype was targeted against before comparing it back to caliper readings.
