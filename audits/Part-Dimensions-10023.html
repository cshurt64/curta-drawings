Derived checks. (1) Both load points lie on one line: 360 gr / 1.8 mm = 500 gr / 2.5 mm = 200 gr per mm, about 1.96 N/mm. (2) Mean coil diameter 1.9 − 0.26 = 1.64 mm; total coils 7.5 = 5.5 active + 2 end, as stated. (3) Textbook spring rate k = G·d⁴ / (8·D³·n) with d = 0.26 mm, D = 1.64 mm, n = 5.5 and an assumed G of 78 GPa for stainless spring steel gives about 1.8 N/mm (about 185 gr per mm), within about 6% of the drawn 200 gr per mm. G is an assumption, not a sheet value. All three are estimates, not sheet facts.

Derived. Scaling every length by 3 with the same material multiplies the spring rate by 3 (k ∝ d⁴/D³ with d and D both ×3) and, at three times the deflection, the force by 9: about 3.2 kgf and 4.5 kgf here, not 360 and 500 gr. A 3× spring that should give the same feel needs its wire, coil count or material re-derived, not just its size. See the project's tolerance strategy.

Open points

  1. Kd = 4500 kg/cm² (Unverified). The symbol is as read; the sheet does not say whether it is an allowable stress or a modulus, and 4500 kg/cm² is far below a shear modulus.
  2. Which load goes with which length (Derived). Read from the drawn graph as 1.8 mm for 360 gr and 2.5 mm for 500 gr, measured from the free-length line.
  3. The scale (Unverified). Only a slash is written in the Maßstab field.
