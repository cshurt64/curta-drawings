Derived: 1.5 + 1.8 + 2 = 5.3 mm, matching the overall height. Seven coils of 0.2 mm wire stack to about 1.4 mm at wire-to-wire contact; the sheet gives 1.8, so the coils are slightly open (Derived).

A rate estimate (not a sheet fact). Textbook torsion-spring stiffness k = E·d⁴ / (10.8·D·n) per radian, with d = 0.2, mean D = 4.8, n = 7 and an assumed E of 190 GPa, gives about 0.84 N·mm/rad, so about 1.9 N·mm at 130°. The sheet's 0.015 kg (about 0.15 N) at a lever arm of, say, the 2 mm leg would be only 0.3 N·mm: roughly six times lower. The sheet does not give the lever arm at which the force is measured, so the two cannot be reconciled from the drawing alone (Unverified).

Derived. A 3× table is not a factory specification. Torsion stiffness goes as d⁴/(D·n): tripling wire and diameter and keeping the coil count multiplies the stiffness by 27 ÷ 3 = 9, and the torque at 130° by 9: a 3× spring in the same steel is nine times stiffer. Its wire and coil count would have to be re-derived from the torque it must give. See the project's tolerance strategy.
