  • Primary source: original Contina engineering drawing, drawing-set PDF page 193 / drawing-viewer page 193, rasterized at 300 and 400 dpi. Dimensions are in µm unless stated (Abmasse in μ = 1/1000 mm); the sheet scale is 10:1.

ISO 286 fit cases

Both fall in the first ISO 286 size step, "up to 3 mm". Neither nominal sits on a step boundary.

Feature Nominal Code Calculation Resulting limits Source
Pin (shaft) Ø0.800 mm h8 IT8 = 14 µm; es = 0, ei = −14 µm Ø0.7860–0.8000 mm 10.021-4
Pin bore (hole) Ø0.800 mm P7 IT7 = 10 µm; ES = −6, EI = −16 µm Ø0.7840–0.7940 mm 10.043-6

For h, es = 0 and the zone lies below nominal; for P (a hole), ES < 0 and the zone also lies below nominal.

The assembly sheet specifies no fit. Each component sheet states a zone for its own feature, and the 10'021 leader on 10.203-1 identifies the pin as seating in the roll. Pairing the two zones is therefore a Derived combination of two Confirmed callouts, not a fit printed anywhere:

Quantity Calculation Value
Maximum clearance hole max − shaft min = 0.7940 − 0.7860 +8 µm
Maximum interference shaft max − hole min = 0.8000 − 0.7840 16 µm
Character clearance possible and interference possible transition

Using the sheet's own fit-table reading for the hole (−7/−16, Ø0.7840–0.7930) the maximum clearance becomes +7 µm (Unverified reading). A transition fit means an individual pin can seat loosely or tightly, so this is not a guaranteed press fit even though P7 is normally a press-fit hole class.

The 4 mm callout and the 3.9 mm pin do not quite agree. They differ by 0.1 mm and only touch at the extreme of both tolerances. Dates offer an explanation but not a proof: 10.203-1 was drawn on 23.2.50 with no revision index, while 10.021-4 was revised on 1.5.1950 (index 3→4, change 163). If the earlier pin index was 4.0 mm long, the assembly callout may simply predate the change. Unverified; the pin sheet is the later, more specific source for the pin's own length.

Project 3× reference

Convention used here: the project's tolerance strategy (a project decision) ties tolerance to the manufacturing process, not to the 3× factor. The "simply scaled tolerance" column is a comparison only and is not adopted for any process. A 3× table is not a factory specification. Where a callout carries an ISO fit code, the code is re-applied at the scaled basic size and the limits recalculated, matching the 10.041 audit.

Both scaled diameters stay in the same ISO size step as the originals, so the derived transition band (+8 µm clearance to 16 µm interference) is unchanged at 3×. A printed FDM roll cannot hold it; per the project's tolerance decision printed prototype fits come from measurement, and a machined or ground roll and pin would hold near the factory absolute band.
