10.207-2 Complete Five-Point-Star Axle — assembly dimensional audit and 3× reference

Scope and source status

  • Assembly: 10.207, Kompl. Fünfzackachse (complete five-point-star axle; the catalog calls it the transmission shaft), current drawing 10.207-2, replacing 10.207-1. Index 1 was changed to 2 on 2.6.50 (change no. 91); what changed is not stated. Identity agrees with Drawing-Index.md and the title block on drawing-set/catalog page 198 (viewer page 198). Scale 5:1 for the elevation and 50:1 for the tooth section and the inspection sketch; stamped Abmasse in μ = 1/1000 mm. Drawn 2.6.1960 (initials as read: schär.). Read at 300 dpi on 2026-09-23.
  • Parent and quantity: 10.236 Kompl. Treibelement IV (complete drive element 4) (Kompl. Nr.); Stück p. Masch. 12 in the title block, and 10.236 is 12 per machine (10.210 sheet 11 roll-up, p213), each holding one. The other three transmission shafts are 10.207, 10.208, 10.209 and 10.216: 12 + 1 + 3 + 1 = 17 of each of the two parts per machine.
  • Assembly fields: general linear tolerance ±50 µ; general angular tolerance a dash; radial (*) and side runout ≤20 µ where marked, none stated for unmarked surfaces; predominant surface finish three triangles; material, surface treatment and heat treatment blank. Type I, group Grundkörper. Edges deburred and broken about 0.1 mm unless noted.
  • Labels: printed controls Confirmed; child-sheet dimensions Confirmed at their stated revisions; arithmetic Derived; unresolved readings Unverified; readings that rest on a judgment Inferred. 3× values are geometric references, not factory specifications.

No ISO fit code appears on this sheet: every limit is an explicit ± or +/− value, so no fit is assigned. ISO 286-1 defines the fit system and ISO 286-2 tabulates deviations; the child sheets' codes are in their own audits.

Drawn date (Unverified). The title block reads Gezeichnet 2.6.1960, but the change entry on the same sheet (index 1 in 2, change 91) is dated 2.6.50, and 10.208-2/10.209-2 read 2.6.1950 for the same drawing date. A sheet cannot be drawn ten years after its own revision; 1960 is very likely a slip for 1950. Scanned at 300 dpi and read the same way each time.

No relief groove

Unlike 10.208-2, 10.209-2 and 10.216-2, this axle carries no relief groove: the elevation shows only the 49.6 length and the 0.2 × 45° chamfer at the free end. Everything else matches the other three sheets. The 10.014-7 axle sheet shows no groove either, so this is the one variant that agrees with it as drawn (Inferred, since the grooves of the other three may simply be omitted there).

Inherited component dimensions

See Part-Dimensions-10014.md, Part-Dimensions-10052.md and Part-Dimensions-105254.md. In brief: axle Ø2 h7, 56 ±0.2 overall, 4 % tapered end, 90° V groove; star bore Ø2 0/−20 with a 4 % taper, five teeth at 72°, body Ø3.8 ±20 and overall 6.95 (agreeing with this sheet).

Derived stacks

Star (Derived). Body 4.35, so the Ø3 toothed head is 6.95 − 4.35 = 2.60 mm tall. If 6.1 is the head's top face, the teeth stand 0.85 mm above it (Inferred).

Axle inside the star (Derived, Inferred). The axle is 56 ±0.2 (10.014-7) and its end is 49.6 ±0.1 below the star's lower face, so about 56 − 49.6 = 6.4 mm of axle lies inside the star (6.1–6.7 mm with tolerances). It assumes the star's lower face is the plane the 49.6 starts from, as the drawing shows.

Press-in. A 0.4 mm travel on two 4 % tapers changes the diametral fit by about 0.016 mm if 4 % means 0.04 mm of diameter per mm of length (Unverified definition). The force of 70 kg is about 686 N if it is a kilogram-force (Inferred).

The 50° tooth tolerance (Unverified)

The tooth section reads 50° 0/−1° here, as on 10.216-2; 105.254 reads +1°/0 for the same flank. The sign is not settled; see Part-Dimensions-10216.md for the discussion. It matters for how the tooth flank is dimensioned on a printed replica, not for the 3× geometry.

Convention used here: the project's tolerance strategy and project decisions tie tolerance to the manufacturing process, not to the 3× factor. "Simply scaled" values are a comparison only and are not adopted; the general ±50 µ does not carry over silently; angles do not scale.
