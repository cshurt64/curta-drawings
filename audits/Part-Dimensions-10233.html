This sheet carries no dimension. There is not a single linear, angular, diametral or radial callout anywhere on it, and — unlike almost every other sheet in the set — it carries no Abmasse in μ = 1/1000 mm stamp, which is consistent: with no dimensions there is no unit to declare. It is a pure arrangement drawing: what goes where, and in what order up the shaft. Nothing on it can be scaled or measured off for fabrication. Every dimension that bears on this assembly comes from a child sheet, and the two that matter most for the envelope are collected under Inherited component dimensions below. A previous version of the side-panel annotation carried a "dimensions in μ" note and an Abmasse in μ label; both were wrong for this sheet and are removed.

No ISO fit code appears on this sheet, and none is assigned to any interface it shows. ISO 286-1 defines the fit system and ISO 286-2 tabulates deviations; the child sheets' codes are in their own audits.

How each quantity was fixed

Per Where-Used-Method.md, a leader count is the weakest channel and is not used here for any quantity. Before this audit, six of the twelve rows in Assembly-Links.csv rested on "single leader on p240", and one was blank.

  • 10.097 — was blank (Unverified), now 9. The leader lines still cannot be counted off the scan, and they do not need to be: the BOM gives 9 spring rings per machine with the remark f. Fünfzackachse, the part's own sheet names 10.233 as its only parent, and this assembly occurs once per machine. Nine per machine with one parent occurring once per machine is nine here. Note that 9 spring rings serve 17 shafts, so they are not one per shaft — which is exactly why deriving the count from the shaft count would have been wrong.
  • VSM 12140 M1×2 — was 1, corrected to 3. The BOM's Bemerkungen column names the assembly the screws belong to (Abdeckring); the total is 3; the cover ring is 1 per machine and sits only in 10.233; the plan view shows three countersunk heads spaced about 120° apart on the cover-ring band; and 10.071-2's own M1:1 view (p93) draws three screw holes. Four readings, all 3. The old value came from counting the one leader line that happens to be drawn.
  • 10.071, 10.137, 10.138, 10.241 — stayed 1, but on better evidence. Each child's own sheet names 10.233 in its Kompl. Nr. field, and each has a per-machine total of 1 against a parent that occurs once per machine. 10.071-2 additionally carries Stück p.Masch. 1 in its own title block, which is the most direct reading available for any part on this sheet.
  • VSM 12707 M1,4 — stayed 1, on arithmetic. 8 per machine, and the three recorded parents take 6 + 1 + 1. That closes exactly and is the only allocation among the recorded parents that does.

The elements form a single closed chain of 17 touching bodies around the main axis with exactly one station-width break in it. A 17-fold even division would be 360/17 = 21.18° and would leave no gap at all, so the pitch is deliberately tighter than even division. ≈20.1° measured is within the scan's uncertainty of 20.0° = 360/18, i.e. an eighteen-station division with one station left open (Inferred; the sheet does not dimension the pitch and the alternative — a pitch chosen directly to clear the screw — reads the same on the scan).

Why the gap is there is not established. One of the three cover-ring countersunk screws lies at nearly the same angle as the break (≈217° against a break centred on ≈215°), which invites the reading that the gap is screw clearance — but the screw sits at about 1.23 × the station radius, outside the chain rather than through it, and the other two screws lie over occupied stations. Screw clearance therefore does not explain the gap on its own, and the near-coincidence at one position may be exactly that. Recorded as Unverified; the sheet says nothing about it.

No absolute scale can be recovered from this sheet. The title block says 3:1, but the scanned page is A5 in PDF units against an original that was certainly larger, so the scan is reduced by an unknown factor, and the sheet carries no dimension to calibrate against. Ratios between features on the plan view are meaningful; absolute millimetres from it are not. A 3× build takes no geometry from this page.

3× reference

There is nothing on this sheet to scale. For completeness, the one inherited envelope dimension:

Feature Factory (10.071-2) Mathematically scaled 3× nominal Simply scaled tolerance Newly calculated ISO tolerance at 3×
Cover-ring outside diameter Ø35 h7, 0/−25 µm ⇒ Ø34.975–35.000 Ø105.000 0/−75 µm ⇒ Ø104.925–105.000 h7 at the 80–120 mm step is IT7 = 35 µm, es = 0 ⇒ Ø104.965–105.000

The three columns are different things and the project must say which it is using. ISO 286-1 fixes es = 0 for h, so the zone lies below nominal in both cases; ISO 286-2 tabulates IT7 = 25 µm at the 30–50 mm step (giving the factory value) and IT7 = 35 µm at the 80–120 mm step (giving the recalculated 3× value). Derived; recalculated here rather than taken from any supplied table. A simply scaled ±, and the 3× figures generally, are geometric references, not adopted fabrication tolerances — see the project's tolerance strategy for the project's per-process policy. The sheet's own general tolerances are dashes and therefore do not scale or carry over at all.

Open points

  1. 10.241 name conflict (Unverified, not resolved here). The part's own current sheet (10.241-1, p249) is titled Kompl. Quotienteneinsteller (complete quotient setter). 10.210 sheet 11 (p213) lists the same drawing number as Kompl. Umschalthebel (complete reversing lever). Both are printed, neither is hand-amended, and the surrounding rows on the roll-up (10.240 Kompl. Einstellgriff, 10.242 Kompl. Rechenmaschine) match the drawing set exactly, so this is not a row-alignment slip. The same two names swap again one level down: BOM sheet 4 (p204) calls 10.066 Quotienteneinstellgriff while Machine-Facts §4 records it as Umschaltgriff. This is the same class as Machine-Facts open item 7 (10.068 Spacer Ring vs Reversing Ring) and is recorded there rather than decided here. The annotations use the drawing's own title, since this is a drawing audit.
  2. VSM 12707 remark vs. allocation (Unverified). BOM sheet 9's Bemerkungen for the M1.4 hex nut reads Nullpuffer (zero buffer), but the three recorded parents are 10.232, 10.233 and 10.244, none of which is the 10.225 complete zero-buffer lever. The arithmetic closes at 8 and the quantity here is not in doubt; what the remark is pointing at is.
  3. Three hexagons on the plan view (Unverified). They sit outside the screw circle and are not individually leadered. 10.232-2 (p239) already accounts for six M1.4 nuts and 10.233-1 for one, so at least some of the three belong to the child assembly. The scan does not separate them.
  4. A second inner fastener group (Unverified, not transcribed). The plan view shows three further small features at roughly half the drive-element radius, one drawn with a screwdriver slot. They carry no leader on this sheet, so no Assembly-Links.csv edge is recorded for them. 10.074 Senkschraube (Ms 58 Pb Ø2, 3 per machine, BOM sheet 4) would fit the count and appearance, but that is a guess and is recorded as one.
  5. Change 241 content (Unverified). The change entry reads only Nummer in Index 1 geändert — the number was changed at index 1. What else, if anything, differs from the unsuffixed original is not stated, and the superseded sheet is not in the set.

A title-block convention worth recording

The Gruppe field on this sheet reads Grundkörper Stufe III while the title reads Grundkörper Stufe II. That is not an error: Gruppe names the parent assembly's name, and this sheet's Kompl. Nr. is 10.234, whose own title (p241) is Grundkörper Stufe III. The same holds one level down — 10.239's Gruppe is Grundkörper Stufe II, and its parent is this sheet. Reading Gruppe as a description of the sheet's own part will produce an off-by-one-level error on every sheet in this branch. Confirmed on three sheets (p240, p241, p247); that it generalises to the whole set is Inferred. Later caveat (2026-09-23): the 10.230 sheet reads Grundkörper under a parent titled Kompl. Treibelement II, so this is a pattern on some sheets, not a rule; Gruppe is best read as the group a sheet is filed under.
