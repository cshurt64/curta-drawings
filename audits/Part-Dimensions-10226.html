Every sheet with a small-size fit table follows one pattern

Across the sheets read so far (10.040-5, 10.095-2, 10.149, 10.150, 10.008-4, and 10.043-6/10.045-6 in their own audits), the entries for sizes up to 3 mm show:

Grade at ≤ 3 mm Zone width on the drawings ISO 286-2
6 7 µm (h6 0/−7) 6 µm
7 9 µm (h7 0/−9, M7 0/−9, N7 −4/−13, P7 −7/−16, S7 −13/−22) 10 µm
8 14 µm (E8, F8, H8) 14 µm

Fits at larger sizes (H7 Ø12, H8 Ø20 and Ø47.8, js9 Ø27) match ISO exactly. This is a consistent, repeated difference confined to ≤ 3 mm and grades 6–7, not a run of random misreadings. It points to a tolerance table other than today's ISO 286-2 for the smallest sizes; the sheets are from 1947–1950 and would have used an older, ISA-based Swiss (VSM) system. That cause is Unverified; the pattern itself is Confirmed as read. An earlier note in the 10.243 audit said the differences were not table-wide; that was wrong, because it looked only at the H8 entries, where the two systems agree anyway.

For the replica this does not matter for what the project does (it re-applies ISO codes at the scaled size, per the project's tolerance decision), but it means the factory's own limits at these sizes are the sheet values, and ISO recalculations are a reference, not a reconstruction of them.

Project 3× reference

Convention used here: the project's tolerance strategy (a project decision) ties tolerance to the manufacturing process, not to the 3× factor. The "simply scaled" values are a comparison only and are not adopted. Where a callout carries an ISO code, it is re-applied at the scaled basic size and the limits recalculated. A 3× table is not a factory specification, and the sheet's own sub-3 mm zone widths are not carried over.

Note on the press-fit rows: the ISO fundamental deviations for S and N at the scaled sizes were not looked up in this pass, so those rows carry the scaled nominal only and no limits. S7 is a press-fit zone at every size, so the pairing stays a press fit in kind; the interference values are not stated.

None of this is a fabrication tolerance. A printed FDM part cannot hold any of it; per the project's tolerance decision the printed prototype fits are set from measurement.
