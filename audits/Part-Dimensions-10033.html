The general ±0.05 mm on a 0.15 mm strip is ±33% of the thickness, so the sheet does not control the spring rate through the thickness tolerance; the Brinell hardness (230–240) is the only material control given (Derived).

3× reference

No ISO fit is recalculated. A leaf spring is not a pure geometric scaling problem, so the table gives the scaled geometry and the consequence.

Feature Factory Scaled 3× nominal Simply scaled tolerance Newly calculated ISO tolerance at 3×
Length 12.7; width 3.5 / 2.9 ±0.05 mm 38.1; 10.5 / 8.7 mm ±0.15 mm not applicable
Fork slot 1.4; eye 1.5 ±0.05 mm 4.2; 4.5 mm ±0.15 mm not applicable

Radii r11.2, r1.75, r0.7, r0.5 ±0.05 mm 33.6; 5.25; 2.1; 1.5 mm ±0.15 mm not applicable

Derived. A 3× table is not a factory specification. Bending stiffness of a leaf goes as w·t³/L³; tripling all three multiplies the stiffness by 3 (and the force at the same relative deflection by 3), so a 3× copy in the same steel is three times stiffer, not the same. The strip thickness (0.45 mm) and hardening would have to be re-derived, and 0.45 mm hardenable stainless strip is a stock size. See the project's tolerance strategy.
